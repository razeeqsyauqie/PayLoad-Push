    How it plays

    The arena

    7×7 board. Your spawn is the back row. The point is the 3×3 square in the middle. The centre tile counts as 2 heroes.

    Both teams field the same five heroes for now: Reinhardt, Tracer, Genji, Ana and Mercy.

    Your back row is safe. Heroes standing on their own spawn row can't be attacked, slept or charged.

    Your turn

    1. Pick one hero.
    2. Move it using its pattern (optional).
    3. Play one of that hero's cards from your hand to attack or use a skill (optional).
    4. Or swap a card you can't use instead.

    Your deck is 25 cards, 5 per hero, all shuffled together. You hold 4 and draw back up to 4 at the end of your turn. No card for a hero means that hero can only move.

    Fights

    Attack = Power + 1 for each of your other heroes next to the target (max +2), +1 if Mercy is next to the attacker.

    Defence = Power + 1 for each ally next to it (max +2), +1 next to Reinhardt.

    Attack ≥ Defence: knocked out. It respawns on its back row after missing one turn. Nobody is gone for good.

    Scoring

    The point unlocks on turn 3. From then on, at the end of every turn, whoever has more heroes on the point gains 10%. A tie is contested and nobody gains.

    100% wins the round. After 10 turns each, the leader wins it. First to 2 rounds wins. The round loser moves first next round.

    Sample turn

    Red's Reinhardt holds the centre tile. Your Ana plays Sleep Dart from 3 tiles away, so he defends with a bare 6. Next turn Genji dashes in beside him and plays Swift Strike, with your Tracer already next to Reinhardt and Mercy next to Genji: 4 + 1 + 1 = 6 against 6. Knockout, and Genji's Reset lets him step onto the centre tile.

    The gold and red badges on the board show this maths before you commit.

    Compared to Mayhem

    Mayhem (the placement mode) is a puzzle: heroes stay where they land and you score shapes. This version is about tempo and threats. Heroes keep moving, fights decide who holds the point, and every hero plays differently.

    Tradeoff: more to learn per hero, and turns take longer to read.
